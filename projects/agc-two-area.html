Automatic Generation Control in a Two-Area Interconnected Power System Using Simulink

MATLAB/Simulink Load Frequency Control Two-Area AGC Power System Stability

Modeled and analyzed a two-area interconnected power system in Simulink to characterize the dynamic inter-dependency between load disturbances, system frequency excursions, and tie-line power deviations. Implemented and evaluated Automatic Generation Control (AGC) with frequency bias and Area Control Error (ACE) under static, step, and variable load conditions.

Context & objectives

In an interconnected power system, maintaining the system frequency at its nominal value and keeping the scheduled interchange power between control areas is essential for reliable operation. AGC achieves three primary objectives: holding system frequency at or very close to nominal, maintaining correct interchange power between control areas, and dispatching generation at the most economic value.

This project focused on the case where instability arises from load variations — sudden, large changes in power demand that unbalance generation and consumption. Two fundamental feedback loops govern the system: Load Frequency Control (LFC) for active power–frequency balance and Automatic Voltage Regulation (AVR) for reactive power–voltage control.

Key contributions

  • Built a complete two-area Simulink model with governor, turbine, rotating mass + load, and ACE-driven integral control blocks for both areas.
  • Derived and implemented the frequency bias factor Bi = 1/Ri + Di and the Area Control Error ACEi = ΔPij + BiΔωi to coordinate control across the tie line.
  • Analyzed five distinct operating scenarios — with and without tie-line coupling, step load changes in Area 1 and Area 2, and variable time-varying load — to isolate the effects of AGC, tie-line support, and disturbance location.
  • Quantified steady-state frequency deviation, settling time, and tie-line power flow to evaluate stability and control effectiveness.

Methodology

The system was analyzed with the transfer-function (Laplace) approach for the linearized model. Each subsystem was modeled as a first-order transfer function and connected in the standard AGC feedback architecture:

  • Generator (swing equation): ΔΩ(s) = (1 / (2Hs)) · [ΔPm(s) − ΔPe(s)].
  • Generator + load: ΔΩ(s) = (1 / (2Hs + D)) · [ΔPm(s) − ΔPL(s)].
  • Prime mover (turbine): GT(s) = 1 / (1 + τTs).
  • Speed governor: Gg(s) = 1 / (1 + τgs) with droop 1/R feeding back Δω.
  • Integral controller (KI/s): added in the feedback loop to drive Δω to zero in steady state for the isolated single-area case.
  • Tie-line power flow: ΔP12 = Ps(Δδ1 − Δδ2), with synchronizing coefficient Ps.

Test scenarios

  • Case 1: Two-area AGC with sudden load applied in Area 2 — observes coupling via the tie line.
  • Case 2: Two-area AGC without tie line, load in Area 2 — isolates Area 2 response.
  • Case 3: Load in Area 1 without tie line — single-area response with integral control.
  • Case 4: Load in Area 1 with tie line — evaluates cross-area support and ACE coordination.
  • Case 5: Variable (time-varying) load in Area 1 with tie line — stress-tests the controller under repeated disturbances.

Outcomes & evaluation

  • Frequency regulation: Integral control in the AGC loop drives steady-state frequency deviation to zero after each disturbance.
  • Tie-line coordination: Frequency bias factors B1 and B2 correctly allocate the disturbance response between the two areas via ACE.
  • Effect of tie line: Presence of the tie line significantly reduces peak frequency deviation and shortens settling time compared to isolated operation.
  • Variable-load robustness: The controller maintains bounded frequency excursions and returns to scheduled interchange under repeated disturbances.
  • Analytical validation: Simulation results align with the analytical steady-state values derived from the transfer-function model.
